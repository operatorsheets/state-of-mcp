The tool surface

8,629 tools across a random sample of 476 live servers (≈194,260+ ecosystem-wide). Only 18% declare an output contract — the rest return whatever they return. 46% of servers expose at least one tool pair an agent could plausibly confuse.

Contracts drift, and it clusters

4.4% of servers changed a tool contract within 36 hours; only 3 more in the next 36. A server can be perfectly up and no longer do what your agent learned it does.

Method: every active remote entry in registry.modelcontextprotocol.io, deduplicated by URL, sent one anonymous JSON-RPC initialize (transport-aware; SSE handled). Tool figures from a seeded random sample so re-runs are comparable. Full method and per-finding write-ups: The Ops Log. This page is regenerated from live data; single snapshots are moments, not truth.

Known limits of this sample, stated because you cannot check them yourself. The drift series re-measures a fixed seeded sample drawn from servers that answered on 2026-07-30. That has two consequences we would rather you knew than discovered. Servers registered after that date have never been sampled, and servers that were down that day are permanently excluded — so as time passes this measures an ageing cohort rather than the registry as it stands. And because the sample is fixed, every figure here describes those servers, not the ecosystem, whenever the two have drifted apart. We are refreshing the population and holding a permanent control slice so the two can be compared rather than conflated; until that ships, read every drift number as cohort, not ecosystem.
